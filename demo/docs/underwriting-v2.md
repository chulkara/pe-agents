# Underwriting v2

The company remains the research record. A saved underwriting version is an immutable acquisition case linked to that company's financial evidence. This release uses deterministic financial calculations and analyst-entered assumptions; it does not require model API credits, an accounting connection, or a new paid service.

## Working process

1. Open **Deals & operations → Underwriting** and select a company. In the local demo, **Load illustrative case** fills explicitly labeled assumptions for exploration. It does not establish financial facts about a real company.
2. In **Reconcile**, choose annual financial sources for revenue, unadjusted EBITDA, D&A, and year-end operating working capital, cash, and existing debt. Keep reporting years separate. Conflicting observations require an explicit selection and reconciliation explanation. Missing values stay unknown until selected or entered as explained assumptions.
3. Map an unrecognized amount to an exact excerpt in a current company document. Confirm the metric, USD units, reporting period, amount, and location. This is a human mapping, not independent verification. Replacement documents invalidate earlier source selections; prior saved models retain their original evidence.
4. Record signed EBITDA adjustments as proposed, accepted, or rejected. Only accepted adjustments affect the earnings base. Positive amounts add back expenses; negative amounts capture recurring or replacement costs. Duplicate accepted source adjustments are rejected.
5. In **Forecast**, set each acquisition year's volume, pricing, customer/revenue losses, margins, variable costs, fixed-cost growth, additional costs, maintenance/growth capital spending, D&A, and operating NWC. There are separate Base, Downside, and Severe assumptions and exit multiples. The horizon is 1–10 complete acquisition years.
6. In **Financing**, enter enterprise purchase price, fees, the NWC peg, additional close NWC, cash reserve, and seller rollover. Configure up to four ordered debt tranches with cash/PIK interest, amortization, interest-only periods, maturities, upfront fees, revolver capacity/commitment fees, and cash sweeps. Inspect sources/uses, individual loan balances, required payments, coverage, and equity funding shortfalls.
7. Use **DCF** for enterprise/equity valuation and sensitivity; **Reverse underwriting** for a price ceiling at fixed financing; and **Downside cases** for simultaneous operating, return, coverage, and funding comparisons.
8. In **Versions & review**, explain changes, confirm reconciliation and assumptions, and choose the case carried into the close/operating plan. Save before review or Excel export. Review records include a named actor, decision, risks, and time. Edits create a new version; reviews do not transfer automatically to later versions.

## Calculation conventions

- All financial amounts are USD. Revenue, reported EBITDA, and D&A are annual flows. NWC, cash, and existing debt are year-end balances. NWC excludes cash and debt. The user must reconcile cash/accrual/tax reporting differences and comparable periods; source amounts are never averaged automatically.
- Adjusted EBITDA equals reported EBITDA plus accepted signed adjustments. Base gross margin and variable-cost assumptions calibrate fixed costs to that earnings base; these cost classifications are assumptions unless independently supported.
- Forecast revenue equals prior revenue × (1 + volume growth) × (1 + price growth) × (1 − customer/revenue loss). EBITDA follows gross profit, variable/fixed costs, and separately entered operating costs. Working-capital cash investment is the change in the modeled balance; additional NWC funded at close increases the opening balance.
- Seller equity consideration equals enterprise price + acquired cash − existing debt + delivered NWC − the agreed peg. Existing debt payoff is a separate use and acquired cash a separate source. Transaction/financing fees, additional NWC, and the cash reserve are separately funded. Buyer cash equity is the remaining source. Rollover shares subsequent company equity calls, distributions, and exit proceeds pro rata at original ownership.
- Debt interest uses opening annual balances and fixed rates. PIK capitalizes annually. Amortization supports straight line, annuity, and bullet; eligible loans receive sweeps in the displayed order after revolvers are repaid. Revolver draws occur at year-end and earn interest from the next year. Earlier maturities are cash obligations; maturities in the final hold year are settled in the exit bridge, avoiding double payment.
- CFADS equals EBITDA less levered cash taxes, capital spending, and NWC investment. DSCR divides CFADS by cash interest, commitment fees, and required principal. Voluntary sweeps are excluded. No debt service means unavailable DSCR, not infinite coverage. Cash shortfalls are explicit new equity calls, not assumed committed capital.
- DCF uses FCFF = EBIT − unlevered cash tax + D&A − capital spending − change in NWC, discounted at year-end WACC. Acquisition debt payments do not reduce FCFF. WACC can be entered directly or built from cost of equity, pretax cost of debt, tax, and target market-value debt weight.
- Terminal value supports perpetuity growth or an EBITDA multiple. Perpetuity normalizes final-year operating margins, D&A, and capital spending to terminal growth and calculates terminal NWC investment with that growth rate. WACC must exceed growth and terminal cash flow must be positive. Invalid terminal assumptions leave DCF unavailable while preserving independently valid financing results.
- IRR uses annual buyer equity cash flows including additional calls and exit proceeds. Multiple sign changes or no valid conventional root produce unavailable IRR; hurdle NPV and MOIC remain visible. MOIC includes all buyer equity invested. Exit common equity is floored at zero; debt shortfalls remain warnings.
- Reverse underwriting holds borrowing, fees, rollover, operating assumptions, and exit assumptions fixed. It prices company equity cash flows to the entered return/MOIC and initial buyer-equity budget. Coverage, closing leverage, and permitted equity calls are separate feasibility requirements. A lower bid cannot repair fixed-borrowing coverage failures. DCF value and maximum supported price are distinct outputs.

This is an annual acquisition cash-flow model, not a complete three-statement model. It does not model monthly liquidity/seasonality, floating-rate resets, loss carryforwards, interest-deduction limits, acquisition tax basis step-ups, fee amortization/OID, preferred equity, subsequent dilution, refinancing, guarantees, lender recoveries, or every debt-like/nonoperating item in an enterprise-to-equity bridge. Cash taxes assume positive EBIT after cash and PIK interest is deductible. Confirm these conventions against deal documents and lender terms before using results for a decision.

## Excel export

The browser downloads a seven-sheet `.xlsx`: Summary, Assumptions, Forecast, Financing, Valuation, Financials, and Audit. Inputs are editable blue values, cross-sheet links are green, calculations are dark, and source excerpts/reconciliation reasons remain alongside financial inputs. One authoritative case selector changes the same operating/debt/valuation builds. The app provides simultaneous live case comparisons; the Excel workbook recalculates one selected case at a time.

The workbook contains formulas, frozen headings, case/horizon validation, and reconciliation checks. No macros, external connections, or server uploads are used. Calculation is set to automatic/full on open; formula caches are deliberately omitted rather than showing stale numbers. Open in a calculating spreadsheet application. Excel edits stay in the downloaded workbook and do not sync to Kalling or inherit approval of the exported version.

Workbook structure/styles were authored with Artifact Tool. The application uses a dependency-free OOXML/ZIP exporter driven by the shared formula plan, so users do not need that authoring dependency. The generic package contains no client records. Regression checks cover package structure, formula injection handling, provenance, and the separation of Audit from business builds.

Verification included importing the actual browser download and recalculating with Artifact Tool; app/workbook parity across all three cases; 1- and 10-year horizons; rollover/negative NWC; early debt maturity; a changed later-year driver; preserved historical inputs; and no formula errors in the scanned model. Native desktop Excel was not available for testing. Cloud-authenticated user acceptance testing remains separate from local-demo UI verification.

## Research used

The [AnalystLab resource collection](https://analystlab.notion.site/7506e77391bd45f9846d387af8814f62?v=21a71c9a96704bff9633c6de20b85d1c) was reviewed for relevant accounting, DCF, LBO, working-capital, and model-design material. The collection includes many overlapping resources and material outside this release, such as public-market trading/comps, VC, and advanced capital structures.
