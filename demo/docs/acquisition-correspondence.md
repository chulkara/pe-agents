# Acquisition correspondence

New outreach uses original formal templates, not model-generated prose. No model
request, paid service, or delivery integration is involved in preparing a draft.

## Prepare a new draft

1. Review the current sourced call brief.
2. Open **Outreach** and choose **Direct acquisition inquiry** for an owner or
   authorised representative, or **Request an owner introduction** for a general
   business contact.
3. Confirm the sender name and salutation. A name in a company record is not proof
   of current ownership. No gender, professional title, or personal connection is
   inferred.
4. Select a checked statement and write one or two sentences explaining the
   company-specific reason for the inquiry. Compare that sentence with the source.
   Selecting a source does not independently validate the wording.
5. Prepare the draft, verify the recipient, review every claim, and approve the
   exact version. Edits create a new version and require a new review.

The templates state acquisition interest, include the reviewed reason, and request
a 15-minute introductory telephone call. They use a formal salutation and closing.
They do not insert website quotations, generic praise, personal familiarity,
unverified credentials, available capital, valuation claims, employee guarantees,
or an assumption that the owner is willing to sell.

Evidence stays beside the draft rather than appearing as website copy in the
email. Additional claims introduced during editing still require human checking.
Unresolved correspondence placeholders block approval. The letter channel starts
with an empty postal recipient rather than reusing the company's email address.

## Replace an earlier draft

Open **Research & outreach → Outreach → Prepare a replacement from a formal
template**. Earlier wording is retained in preparation history. A replacement is
unreviewed and does not inherit recipient verification or approval. Existing
saved drafts are not silently rewritten.

Legacy work requests link to this same sourced company workflow. The older local
draft fallback uses formal wording with an explicit company-specific rationale
placeholder; it cannot receive a work-queue approval until that is completed.

## Reference and scope

The supplied *Vault Career Guide to Private Equity*, fifth edition (2023), contains
career correspondence and job-application examples, rather than seller acquisition
templates. Its relevant guidance on concise structure, recipient specificity,
truthful claims, a clear request, and proofreading informed these original
templates. The book and its sample letters are not included in this repository.

Follow-up messages are not generated as if a prior email had been sent: a draft,
approval, or copy does not establish external delivery. This change adds initial
acquisition correspondence only.

## Validation

Tests cover the reviewed-brief requirement, selected evidence, required rationale,
formal wording, postal recipient handling, replacement history, placeholder
blocking, and preservation of review gates. Browser QA uses a fictional local
company and test recipient, not a real owner or delivered message.
